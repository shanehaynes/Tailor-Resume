# The owner's voice (for cover letters)

Read `profile/voice.md`: the owner's voice profile, written during onboarding from
their own writing samples. If it is missing, ask the owner for 2-3 samples of their
writing (essays, posts, long emails, a past cover letter they liked; prose they wrote
alone, not edited by a tool), derive a profile with the method below, show it to
them, and write it to `profile/voice.md` once they approve. Re-derive when they
supply new samples. `profile/voice.example.md` shows the shape.

## Deriving the profile from samples
Record, at the top: the samples used (title, date, where), and which one is the
better guide (usually the most recent and tightest). Then 6-10 traits, each one:
- a name for what the prose **does** (a move, not an adjective: "opens with a
  concrete scene, then states the point", not "clear" or "thoughtful");
- one short verbatim quote from a sample that shows it;
- how often it appears (every paragraph, once per piece, at the climax only).

Ask of the samples, at least (the answer may be "none" or "varies"):
1. **Openings**: how does a piece start? A claim, a story, a question, a number,
   context, something else?
2. **Argument**: how is a point supported? Examples, data, steps, comparison,
   a story, authority?
3. **Rhythm**: how long are the sentences, and how much do they vary?
4. **Figures**: any recurring images, analogies, humor, or none?
5. **Uncertainty and objections**: hedged, addressed, or left out?
6. **Self-reference**: how often and how does the writer speak of themself?
7. **Punctuation**: any habits that carry meaning (lists, dashes, parentheses)?
8. **Endings**: how does a piece close?
9. **Register and diction**: formal or casual, technical or plain; words they
   reach for and words they never use.

Describe only what the samples show. If samples disagree, say so and ask the owner
which one sounds most like them.

## Transposing to a cover letter
`profile/voice.md` has its own "Transposing to a cover letter" section, agreed with
the owner; it decides. Where it is silent:
- The letter argues one idea, the angle's thesis, the way this writer makes a
  point; ledger facts are the evidence, not a list.
- Role and current school or employer appear in the first paragraph.
- Opinions are the owner's to state; claims about what they did come only from
  cited ledger facts.
- Keep the owner's register, adjusted for a business reader.

## Never (generic cover-letter voice)
- "I am writing to express my interest", "I am excited/passionate", "I believe I
  would be a great fit", "unique opportunity", "I am confident that".
- Restating the resume in order. Adjective stacks. Thanking twice.
- Buzzwords (innovative, leverage, ecosystem, transformative, synergy...), plus any
  words the owner's profile or writing skills list as ones to avoid.
