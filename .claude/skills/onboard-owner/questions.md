# Question bank

Ask 2-4 at a time, in the order below within each part. Fill the brackets from
the ledger; quote the exact claim you are asking about. Offer choices when the
answers are knowable (never for numbers, dates, or places: ask for theirs), and
always "not sure" (the question stays open, the fact keeps its status). Record
answers verbatim; each one becomes a ledger edit under the ledger-update skill's
rules.

How an answer lands:
- Confirms a claim as written -> fact `confirmed`, note with their words.
- Corrects it -> new `confirmed` fact; old fact `retired`; variants using the old
  fact are now blocked, so draft a `proposed` variant on the corrected fact if the
  bullet is worth keeping.
- Adds a fact -> new `confirmed` fact; no new wording unless they want it.
- Resolves an open question -> move it to `resolved_questions` with `answer` and
  `consequence`, then apply the consequence.

---

## Part A: Facts

### A1. Blockers (all of them, first)
Conflicts block every build that touches them.
- "Your [version A] says [claim A]; your [version B] says [claim B]. Which is
  true?" Choices: A / B / neither (then: what is true?) / both, in different
  periods (then: which dates?).
- Title or dates differ across versions: "Which is exact: [title 1, dates 1] or
  [title 2, dates 2]? Your offer letter or HR system decides."

### A2. Role by role, most recent first
For each role, in this order. Skip what the ledger already settles. Roles added
since the newest source (Phase 1.6) come first.

**Numbers** (each number on its own; never batch-confirm a number). Ask the
first two for every number. Ask the rest only when the answer was an estimate,
someone else's figure, or a team result.
- "How was [number] measured?" Choices: system or report data / a time study or
  count you did / someone else's figure (who?) / your estimate / a forecast.
- "Over what period, and compared with what?" Per week, per year, one-time; the
  baseline before; annualized or actual.
- "Whose result is it: yours, your team's, or the program's?" If a team's: "What
  part was yours?"
- "Is it realized (measured after the fact) or projected?"
- "Could you show where it comes from if asked?" No -> keep it, but the claim says
  "about" or "estimated", or the number is dropped. Their call.

**Attribution** (the verb is the claim)
- "What exactly did you do on [accomplishment]? If an interviewer asked 'what was
  your part', what would you say?"
- "Choose the verb that is literally true:" led / built / designed / analyzed /
  recommended / coordinated / supported. Then: "Who made the final decision?"
- "How many people were involved, and did anyone report to you?" Direct reports,
  informal leadership, and cross-team coordination are different facts.
- "Who were the stakeholders, by function?" (A "cross-functional" claim needs names
  of functions.)

**Characterizations** an interviewer will test
- Words that overclaim easily: "launched" vs "piloted"; "saved" vs "projected to
  save"; "automated" vs "partly automated"; "built" vs "configured"; "strategy" vs
  "analysis"; "customers" vs internal users. Ask which is true, with the choices.
- "Was it used after you left or after the pilot? By how many people?"

**Confirmation of the rest** (single facts, no numbers)
- Show up to 8 claims for the role, verbatim, numbered: "Are these true as
  written? Reply with the numbers of any that are wrong or overstated." Only the
  ones they affirm become `confirmed`; quote the reply in each note.

**Missing outcomes** (bullets with no result)
- "After [accomplishment], what changed? Time, cost, errors, volume, revenue,
  satisfaction, adoption?" Then the measurement questions above for any number.
- "If you don't know a number, what did someone say about it?" (Praise from a
  named role is a fact; their quote goes in the note.)

### A3. Cross-cutting
**Tools and skills**
- For each inventory item: "In the last few years, did you use it at work /
  build something with it / take a course only / never really?" Course-only and
  never move to `familiar_only` or are retired.
- "What do you use weekly that no resume mentions?"

**Exactness**
- "Official title in the HR system vs the title you use?" Record both; ask which
  ships.
- "Start and end month for each role?" (Owner's call whether months show.)
- "Any gap between roles longer than 3 months? Should it be explained (e.g. a
  caregiving period, a contract) or left as dates?" Record it as a role `note`;
  preferences hold the showing rule.
- "Setting and scope for each role: department or unit type, its size, how many
  people or cases you handled per shift or week, any lead or charge duties?"
  Each answer is a fact, with A2's number questions.
- "Employer descriptor: what is the company in one line, and where does any size
  or rank number come from?"
- Locations: city, remote, hybrid, as they want it shown.

**Education**
- "Official degree, major, minor, concentration names, exactly as on the diploma
  or transcript?" Ask for the document if handy.
- "Honors and scholarships: official names and years?"
- "GPA and test scores: what are they, and should they ever appear?" (Record the
  fact; the showing rule goes in preferences.)
- "Clubs and leadership roles: official names, your title, years?"
- "Any degree, certificate, or course in progress? Official name, institution,
  expected completion (month and year)?"

**Licenses, certifications, and languages**
- "Professional licenses: type, issuing state or body, active or lapsed, expiry,
  multistate?" License numbers are never recorded or shown.
- "Credentials after your name on the resume (letters for a degree or license)?
  Which, in what order?"
- "Is each certification current? Expiry date?"
- "Language level for each:" native / fluent / professional / intermediate / basic.

**True, but don't show**
- "Is there anything here that is true but you never want on a resume? A short
  role, an employer, an old field, a number, a client name under NDA?" Record it
  as a fact note (the fact stays; preferences hold the rule).
- "Any client or employer names you may not use? What may you say instead?"
- Other people's data never enters the ledger: no patient, client, student, or
  coworker names or identifying details, no health or confidential business
  information. Record the fact as counts and roles, and say why.

### A4. What no source mentions (memory joggers)
Ask 2-3 at a time; every answer that matters gets the A2 questions.
- (Career changers: ask this first, before A2.) "Look at the postings you
  shared. What work have you done, in any role, that resembles it: committees,
  process or quality-improvement projects, audits, scheduling or staffing,
  budgets, rolling out a system or tool, tracking or reporting data, writing a
  policy or procedure, training others? For each: what, when, your part, what
  changed."
- "What are you proudest of that is on no resume?"
- "What problem were you asked to fix because others couldn't?"
- "What did you start without being asked?"
- "What did you teach, train, document, or hand over to others?"
- "Any process, tool, template, or report you created that others still use?"
- "Recognition: awards, early promotions, spot bonuses, being asked back,
  written praise?"
- "What went wrong, and what did you do about it?"
- "Side projects, volunteering, writing, talks, open-source, competitions?"
- "What would your last manager say were your two biggest contributions?"

### From scratch (no prior resume)
1. "List every job, internship, and significant volunteer role: employer, title,
   city or remote, start and end month." (One message; they can paste a list.)
2. Per role, most recent first: "What were the 3-5 things you did that mattered
   most?" Then A2 for each.
3. Education, certifications, languages, tools (A3).
4. A4 joggers.
Every fact is `confirmed` (their words). Draft variants as `proposed`; read each
back and change it until they approve it word for word.

## Part B: Preferences (Phase 4)
Each answer fills a slot in `profile/preferences.example.md`. Most people have no
opinion on most slots, so start from defaults:
1. Write a draft `profile/preferences.md` with these defaults: US Letter;
   contact items phone, email, LinkedIn; no location line; standard headings;
   skills inside Additional Information; descriptors shown; the degree variant
   tagged `default`, single line; GPA and test scores never; precise-act verbs;
   US spelling; "about 40%"; finished PDFs copied nowhere; recruiter skim yes;
   writing skills to load: none (the owner may add names later).
2. Ask only the questions below marked **(ask)**, plus Targets if Phase 1 did
   not record them.
3. Show the draft: "Change anything?" The other questions below are what to ask
   about a slot the owner wants to change.

**Targets** (already asked in Phase 1; confirm, don't re-ask)
- "Which roles are you applying for? Titles, as postings write them."
- "Industries or company types?" (sector, size, stage)
- "Level?" intern / new graduate / early career / mid / senior / leadership.
- "Locations: cities, remote, relocation?"
- "Your recruiting window and any known deadlines?"

**Positioning** (ask)
- "In one sentence, the story you want a reader to take away." (Their words;
  Phase 1 may have it already: confirm.)
- "Two to four strengths you want to be known for; anything to downplay?"
- Reminder: the angle for each application is still their choice every time.

**Resume content**
- "Your name as it should appear on the resume?" (preferred or legal; with any
  credentials after it, A3).
- "Contact items to show, in order:" phone, email, location (city and state, or
  remote), LinkedIn, GitHub, website, blog.
- "Section order:" education first (students, recent graduates, career changers
  in a program) / experience first.
- "Headings:" standard (EDUCATION, PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE, PROJECTS, ADDITIONAL
  INFORMATION) or their wording; a separate SKILLS section or skills inside
  Additional Information?
- "Summary section at the top?" Not supported yet (a template change); say so.
- "Degree line:" single line / two lines (official title on its own line), and
  which degree variant.
- "Employer descriptors (the italic line: what the company is):" show / hide.
- "GPA:" never / only if a posting asks / always. Same for test scores.
- **(ask)** "Anything never to list?" (tools, a role, interests, a certification)
- "Anything to list only when the posting names it?"
- "Interests line:" yes / no / only for some targets.

**Wording**
- "When a bullet could say 'owned' or 'drove' or instead name the act ('designed',
  'negotiated', 'built'), which do you prefer?" precise act / stronger ownership
  word when a fact supports it / no preference.
- "Words you never want used?" (Offer a starter list: spearheaded, leveraged,
  synergy, passionate, results-driven, innovative.)
- "Spelling:" US / UK. "Approximations:" `~40%` / `about 40%` / avoid.
- "Dashes, Oxford comma, '%' vs 'percent'?" (Only if they care.)

**Output**
- Page size: US Letter (the only one supported; A4 is a template change).
- **(ask)** "Where should finished PDFs be copied?" none / ~/Downloads / another folder.
- "File name for that copy?" `resume-<company>.pdf` (the default) /
  `<Firstname>-<Lastname>-Resume.pdf` / other. The application folder always keeps
  `resume-<company>.pdf`.
- **(ask)** "Do you want cover letters?" always / when the posting asks / never.
- If yes: "Sign-off name?"
- "Recruiter-skim review (a fresh reviewer reads each tailored resume as that
  company's recruiter and says what it notices):" keep (recommended) / skip.

**Workflow** (from Phase 0): parallel or single; confirm it still holds.

## Part C: Voice (Phase 5, only with cover letters)
- "Send 2-3 things you wrote alone and like: essays, posts, long emails,
  statements of purpose. Not AI-drafted, not edited heavily by others."
- "Which one sounds most like you at your best?"
- "Any writers or publications whose style you want to sound like, or not like?"

No samples: ask them to write, in chat, 120-200 words each:
1. "Explain a piece of work you're proud of to a smart friend outside your field."
2. "Argue for an opinion you hold about your industry."
3. "Write the email you'd send a hiring manager you met once, asking for 20 minutes."
Save each answer as a sample with its prompt.

After the draft profile: "Does this sound like you? Which trait is wrong or
missing? Which 'never' would you add?"
